## Method findings — why the metadata approach failed

**1. The Olympus E-510 has no GPS receiver, yet its frames carry coordinates.** Photos
assigned them. In `Pennsylvania`, E-510 frames dated 2009-10-25 read 32.82/-96.72 (Dallas)
on the same day Canon frames read 41.21/-77.20 (Pennsylvania). Treat any E-510 coordinate
as absent, not as evidence.

**2. The GPS median test fails on compound albums.** `Louisville and Seattle` read as
Kansas, which is just the midpoint of two real cities plus home.

**3. The GPS median test is biased toward snapshots.** The cameras that made the real work
(X-T5, GFX100S II, a6000, X-E2, E-M10) have no GPS. The iPhone does. So the median lands
wherever the phone was, and systematically undercounts photographic work. `Quanah` has 15
real frames and its GPS median points at the house in Frisco.

**4. Content beats coordinates.** `Atlanta` has ONE frame with GPS and eleven real frames.
The Georgia Aquarium is unmistakable on sight and invisible to metadata.

**5. The ">=10 frames per contiguous block = real visit" threshold is too blunt.** It
wrongly zeroed `Marble Falls` (11 real frames) and `San Diego` (7 real frames).

## Consequence for the rebuild plan

**The GPS-first rebuild proposed earlier in this session is the wrong plan.** GPS correlates
with the iPhone, and the iPhone correlates with snapshots. Anchoring on GPS would weight the
structure toward family photos and away from the X-T5, GFX and a6000 work, which is backwards
from what this archive is for.

The only method that worked today was rendering contact sheets and looking at them. It took
about 90 seconds per album and it was right every time, including on albums with zero GPS.

Recommended: contact-sheet every remaining album before any keyword is written. The sheets
are cheap to generate (`scripts/python/sheets.py`) and they are the only signal that did not
lie.
